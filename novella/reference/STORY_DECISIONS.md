## Human Gate 0 - Approved Direction

*Decisions captured December 2024*

## Final Decisions

### Decision 1: Frame Story
**Choice: Option A - Pure Historical**
- No Harry frame
- Story stands completely on its own
- HPMOR connections are thematic, not explicit

### Decision 2: The Binding
**Choice: Option A - Climactic Ending**
- The story ENDS with the binding that begins the Statute of Secrecy
- Arthur and Merlin together make the choice to formalize wizard-Muggle separation
- Aftermath chapters serve as epilogue showing the consequences
- This gives the story a definitive, world-changing endpoint

### Decision 3: Philosopher's Stone
**Choice: Option B - Subtle Easter Egg**
- The Stone is hinted at, never named explicitly
- To general readers: a mysterious magical artifact Merlin possesses
- To HPMOR readers: recognizable easter egg
- Should be mentioned 2-3 times maximum, obliquely

### Decision 4: POV Distribution
**Choice: Option B - Weighted by Section**
- Part One: Mostly Arthur (investigation mode)
- Part Two: Shift toward Merlin (revelation mode)
- Part Three: Balanced/dual scenes (integration mode)
- Serves story needs rather than forced alternation

### Decision 5: Arthur's Age
**Choice: 14, going on 15**
- Younger than originally recommended
- Emphasizes the "boy king" element
- Creates larger gap with Merlin's ancient wisdom
- Allows for growth and coming-of-age arc
- Voice must be adjusted: brilliant but young, idealistic but inexperienced
- Makes his rationalism more remarkable (prodigy) and his mistakes more forgivable

### Decision 6: Interludes
**Choice: Worldbuilding Flavor, Not Plot-Critical**
- Fun, scary, heartwarming, intriguing side stories
- Add texture and depth to the world
- NOT required to understand the main plot
- Can be skipped without losing story thread
- Plan for ~4-6 interludes scattered throughout

### Decision 7: Excalibur's Purpose
**Choice: Sword of Borrowed Certainty (Prophecy-Driven)**
- Excalibur grants its wielder strategic/tactical certainties—knowledge without source
- Arthur *knows* things he has no reason to know (e.g., Saxon invasion timeline)
- The certainty feels borrowed, foreign, manipulative to Arthur
- Cannot distinguish between his own conclusions and the sword's influence

**Why Merlin Created/Gave It:**
- A prophecy told Merlin to place this artifact for whoever drew the sword
- He doesn't know WHY prophecy demanded it
- He resents being prophecy-bound (echoes HPMOR: "Merlin wished to free us from prophecy")
- This resentment eventually drives him to create the Hall of Prophecy

**Arthur's Arc With the Sword:**
- Initially distrusts it completely; views himself as Merlin's pawn
- Suspects the sword is Merlin's tool to control him
- Eventually realizes: the certainties were GOOD—they helped him rule well
- The sword informed him; the choices were still his own
- This becomes a model for his eventual acceptance of Merlin's larger secret:
  - Guidance without full explanation can still be trustworthy

**Prophecy's Irony:**
- Merlin gave the sword because prophecy demanded it
- He didn't realize it would be what teaches Arthur to trust him
- Prophecy worked through Merlin even as he resented it
- Parallels Dumbledore's prophecy-bound manipulation of Harry in HPMOR

### Decision 8: Merlin's Hat → Sorting Hat Origin
**Choice: Memory Storage Device (Must Be Worn)**
- A battered, ancient hat that serves as external memory storage
- Must be WORN to access (direct mind-to-mind contact required)
- Two-way transfer: stores wearer's memories, shares stored memories with wearer
- The hat reads the wearer's question/need, provides relevant memories
- Cannot be queried from a distance or through writing

**What It Contains:**
- Centuries of Merlin's accumulated lore and observations
- Spells, historical knowledge, languages, names of the dead
- Memories of civilizations that no longer exist
- Knowledge too dangerous to write down but too important to forget
- Fragments of Atlantean understanding

**Connection to the Interdict:**
- The hat is a loophole in the Interdict of Merlin
- Powerful magic can't be transmitted in text—but CAN be transmitted through direct mind-to-mind memory transfer
- Merlin exploits this carefully to preserve pre-Interdict knowledge
- One of the few repositories of truly ancient magical understanding

**Developing Personality:**
- Centuries of absorbed thoughts create accretion of opinion
- The hat develops wit, preferences, judgment
- Not truly sentient, but something like personality emerges
- It holds pieces of everyone who ever wore it

**Evolution to Sorting Hat:**
- Hogwarts founders (with Merlin's guidance?) eventually repurpose it
- Already full of Merlin's memories about what makes wizards wise/brave/cunning/loyal
- The hat uses that accumulated wisdom to evaluate students
- The personality is the accretion of centuries of absorbed thoughts
- The requirement to be worn persists: Sorting requires direct contact

## Additional Direction

### Knights of the Round Table

**Research Findings:**
- No single canonical number (ranges from 12 to 150+ depending on source)
- Winchester Round Table lists 25 knights
- Most prominent across sources: Lancelot, Gawain, Galahad, Percival, Bedivere, Kay, Mordred
- The Round Table concept appears in Wace's *Roman de Brut* (1155) - made round so no knight could claim precedence

**Story Decision: Start Small, Grow**
- Begin with Arthur and a handful of loyal followers (5-8)
- The Round Table itself is Arthur's innovation in this story
- Knights join over time as the kingdom grows
- Keep named knights manageable for novella scope

**Key Knight: Sir Lancelot**
- Should become Arthur's best friend
- Enemies-to-friends arc:
  - Arthur assumes Lancelot is typical highborn: uses strength and station to dominate others
  - Lancelot assumes Arthur is typical peasant: dull-minded, scraping at the ground
  - Both are wrong
  - Bond over shared disdain for: fools pretending to be wise, hypocrites, those who confuse power with virtue
  - Lancelot is slightly older than Arthur (maybe 17-18 when they meet)
  - Becomes Arthur's closest confidant (but NOT in on the Merlin secret—that's Arthur's alone)

**Other Knights to Include:**
- **Sir Bedivere** - Already in Chapter 1, military advisor
- **Sir Kay** - Traditional role as Arthur's foster-brother and seneschal
- **Sir Gawain** - Could represent the old nobility accepting Arthur
- Leave room for others to be introduced as needed

### Religion

**Research Findings:**
Christianity in 5th Century Britain:
- Christianity was present from Roman period, continued in western Britain after Rome withdrew
- Emperor Theodosius banned paganism in 391 AD - Christianity official religion
- Anglo-Saxon invasions (mid-5th century) brought pagan gods: Tiw, Woden, Thor, Frey
- British Christians fled to Wales, Cornwall, Brittany; some were martyred
- Celtic Christianity developed distinctly from Roman Church
- Pelagian heresy was active in Britain (denial of original sin)
- St. Patrick (British-born) took Christianity to Ireland in 432 AD
- Religion was largely restricted to higher-status social groups in late Roman Britain

**Story Opportunities:**
- Brother Marcus (already in Chapter 1) represents the Church
- Tension between Christian doctrine and magical reality
- The Church views magic with suspicion (devil's work? or God's gift?)
- Arthur must navigate between Christian advisors and Merlin
- Possible conflict: Church demands Merlin be expelled or executed as sorcerer
- The Interdict could parallel Church's approach to forbidden knowledge
- Celtic Christianity vs. Roman Christianity as background tension
- Pelagian controversy (free will vs. predestination) mirrors the Benefactor's choice

**Thematic Resonance:**
- Religion as another system trying to restrict dangerous knowledge
- The Church's instinct to burn heresy vs. Merlin's quiet restriction
- Both are trying to protect people from ideas that could destroy them
- Arthur caught between faith and evidence, tradition and innovation

**9. Betrayal from Within**

## Implications for Story Architecture

### Arthur's Voice Adjustment

With Arthur at 14-15, his voice needs recalibration:
- Still brilliant and observant (prodigy)
- But more impulsive, emotionally volatile
- Less political sophistication
- More wonder at the world
- Mistakes are more forgivable
- His "rationalism" is precocious but incomplete
- Growth arc is more dramatic (boy to man, not young man to mature leader)

### Lancelot's Role

Lancelot provides:
- A peer relationship Arthur lacks (Merlin is ancient, advisors are older)
- Someone to confide in (but not about the deepest secrets)
- A mirror for Arthur's growth
- Action/adventure subplot potential
- Audience proxy who can ask questions

### Religion Integration

Brother Marcus becomes more important:
- Voice of the Church in Arthur's council
- Genuine faith vs. political religion tension
- Could be sympathetic or antagonistic (or both at different times)
- His arc: from suspicious of Merlin to... what? Acceptance? Exile? Martyrdom?

### Conflict Pacing

Suggested distribution:
- **Part One**: Plague + Legitimacy + Lancelot introduction + Religious tension building
- **Part Two**: Saxons + Dragon? + Religious persecution + Round Table crisis
- **Part Three**: Mirror revelation + Binding + Aftermath
