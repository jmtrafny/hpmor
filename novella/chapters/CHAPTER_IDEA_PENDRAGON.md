# The Weight of Knowing

## Chapter [X]: The Pendragon

---

### Scene Notes

**Context:** This takes place sometime after Chapter One but before the Midpoint Mirror scene. Arthur has established himself as a capable king but is still skeptical of magic and Merlin. This scene:
1. Gives Arthur his legendary surname
2. Shows Arthur's rationalist approach to an "impossible" problem
3. Reveals that Merlin has deeper knowledge than he lets on
4. Sets up the dynamic where Merlin *could* help but chooses to let Arthur learn
5. Plants seeds that Merlin's methods might be superior (no bloodshed vs. bloody victory)

**Period Notes:**
- Post-Roman Britain, so Arthur would have had a simple name. Peasants typically had single names or patronymics (Arthur son of [foster father's name])
- "Pendragon" traditionally means "chief dragon" or "head dragon" in Welsh (pen = head/chief, dragon = dragon/leader)
- Could also interpret as "dragon's head" — what Arthur took as a trophy?

**The Threat:**
- Saxon or Pictish war band has acquired/allied with a dragon
- Dragons are thought extinct on the isles — this one came from the continent? From the north?
- The dragon is their siege weapon, their terror tactic
- Merlin knows things about dragons he shouldn't — hints at his true age/experience

### Draft

The messenger's report was absurd. Arthur read it twice to be certain.

"A dragon," he said flatly.

"Yes, Your Majesty." The messenger — a young man from Lord Brennius's holding in the north — had the hollow-eyed look of someone who had seen something that didn't fit into his understanding of the world. "It came with the Pictish raiders. They... they ride it. Or it follows them. We're not certain."

"Dragons do not exist."

"This one does, Your Majesty."

Arthur set down the parchment. Around the council table, his advisors shifted uncomfortably. Lord Ector was studying the map with exaggerated interest. Lord Bedivere had his hand on his sword hilt, as if a blade might help against a creature that could allegedly melt stone.

"Describe it."

"Larger than a horse. Wings like a bat's, but vast — thirty feet, perhaps more. Scales the color of..." The messenger paused, searching for words. "Like iron left in a fire. Dark, but with red beneath. It breathes fire, Your Majesty. I saw it. I saw it burn—" His voice cracked. "It burned the granary at Dunholm in a single pass. The men inside..."

Arthur waited.

"They didn't even scream, Your Majesty. It was too fast."

The throne room was silent.

Arthur looked at Merlin.

The old wizard stood in his usual place, half-hidden in shadow, his face betraying nothing. But something in his posture had changed — a tension Arthur had learned to recognize over the past months. The same tension he showed when Arthur strayed too close to questions Merlin didn't want to answer.

"You seem unsurprised, wizard."

Merlin's expression didn't flicker. "I have lived a long time, Your Majesty. I have learned not to be surprised."

"Have you seen dragons before?"

A pause. The slightest hesitation before Merlin answered.

"Once. Long ago."

"Where? There are no dragons in Britain."

"No." Merlin's voice was soft. "Not anymore."

Arthur filed that away for later examination. *Not anymore* implied there had been dragons once. Implied Merlin remembered a time before they were gone. How old was this man?

"Can they be killed?" Arthur asked.

"Anything can be killed, Your Majesty. The question is always cost."

"That's not an answer."

"It is the only honest answer I can give." Merlin stepped forward, into the torchlight, and for a moment Arthur saw something in his eyes that might have been respect. Or warning. "Dragons are not beasts, Your Majesty. They are intelligent. Cunning. They remember grudges across generations. If you fight this creature and fail, it will not simply kill you. It will hunt everyone you have ever protected. It will make an example."

"And if I don't fight it?"

"Then the Picts will use it to burn every holding between here and the sea. And the lords will remember that their king did nothing."

Arthur stared at the map. At the northern territories where the dragon had been sighted. At the roads the Pictish war band would take if they pressed south.

"Tell me everything you know about dragons."

Merlin was silent for a long moment.

"They are fire given form," he said finally. "Their breath is not simply flame — it is something closer to the heat of a forge, concentrated and directed. Their scales can turn aside most blades, most arrows. Their eyes are weak points, but they know this and protect them. They fly faster than a horse can run, and they can see movement from a mile away."

"What are they vulnerable to?"

"Cold. Water. Surprise." Merlin paused. "And pride. Dragons are vain, Your Majesty. They believe themselves superior to all other creatures. This can be exploited."

"How?"

Merlin's lips curved into something that wasn't quite a smile.

"That depends on how clever you are."

*[SCENE BREAK: Arthur researches, plans, faces skepticism from advisors]*

Arthur spent three days in the library — such as it was. The Roman texts had been picked over decades ago, anything valuable long since taken or burned. But there were scraps. Fragments. Old bestiaries that mixed fact with fantasy, traveler's accounts that couldn't distinguish between what they'd seen and what they'd been told.

He learned:
- Dragons needed to eat enormous amounts to fuel their fire
- Their flame had limits — they could not breathe continuously
- They were territorial and solitary by nature
- They hated the cold

And he thought:
- The Picts had brought this dragon from somewhere. It wasn't native. It was probably uncomfortable, possibly homesick.
- It was working with humans, which was unusual. Why? Payment? Coercion? Alliance?
- It had been burning buildings, not armies. Soft targets. Terror tactics.

*It's a siege weapon*, Arthur realized. *They're using it the way Rome used catapults. Break the walls, destroy supplies, crush morale before the infantry arrives.*

And siege weapons had counters.

---

*[SCENE BREAK: The battle — Arthur's plan involves something clever with water/cold/terrain]*

**Possible "secret weapon" ideas:**

1. **Greek Fire reversal**: Arthur has learned about Greek Fire from Roman texts. He creates something similar — but designed to *explode* when the dragon's fire hits it. Baits the dragon into breathing on a specific target, which detonates in its face. Not killing it, but blinding and disorienting.

2. **Terrain trap**: Arthur lures the dragon to a specific location where he's prepared a trap — a frozen lake, a flooded quarry, something that will ground it and negate its flight advantage.

3. **Exploit the pride**: Arthur challenges the dragon directly. Makes it angry enough to attack him personally rather than supporting the Pictish infantry. Single combat, with Arthur using something unexpected.

4. **Ballista/siege weapon**: Romans left behind siege equipment. Arthur has modified a ballista to fire something special — chains to foul wings, bolts treated with something that disrupts the fire-breathing.

**Leaning toward combination**: Arthur uses the terrain (a lake/river valley) combined with a baited trap. He's identified the dragon's attack pattern — always leads with fire, always from above. He prepares a position that looks vulnerable but is actually designed to draw the dragon into a kill zone.

The "clutch" moment: The dragon is smarter than expected. It sees the trap and starts to pull away. Arthur does something desperate and brave — makes himself the bait, stands in the open, forces the dragon to commit. The trap springs. The dragon goes down.

But it's not dead. Arthur has to finish it personally. This is where he earns the name — not just defeating the dragon, but facing it at the end, when it's wounded and furious and still dangerous.

---

*[SCENE BREAK: Aftermath — celebration, naming]*

The soldiers called him Pendragon before he returned to the capital.

*Pen* for chief. *Dragon* for what he had slain. Or perhaps for what he had taken from it — the head, cleaned and preserved, that now hung in the great hall as proof that the impossible could be defeated.

Arthur Pendragon. It sounded like something from the old stories. The kind of name that belonged to heroes who fought giants and bargained with gods.

It didn't feel like a victory.

---

*[SCENE BREAK: Merlin's conversation]*

"You did well."

Arthur looked up from the fire. Merlin stood in the doorway of his chambers, older and more tired than usual, but with something in his eyes that might have been approval.

"I did what was necessary."

"Yes." Merlin moved into the room, settling into a chair across from Arthur. The firelight played across his weathered face. "That is what makes it impressive. Necessity, not glory. You could have led a charge. Died heroically. Become a legend."

"Dead legends don't protect their people."

"No. They don't." Merlin was quiet for a moment. "I faced a dragon once. Did I tell you that?"

"You said you'd seen one. You didn't mention facing it."

"Her name was Fancadonda. She terrorized the western coast for three seasons, burning fishing villages and demanding tribute. The local lords tried everything — poison, siege weapons, hired mercenaries. Nothing worked."

"How did you defeat her?"

Merlin's smile was sad and strange.

"I learned that she had lieutenants. Lesser drakes who served her, hunted for her, reported on human movements. I spent two months gaining the trust of one of them. A young male named Terreth who felt overlooked, undervalued. I convinced him that Fancadonda was planning to kill him — that she saw him as a threat to her dominance."

"Was she?"

"No. But dragons are proud, and pride makes them susceptible to doubt. Terreth confronted Fancadonda. She was... intemperate in her response. The other lieutenants saw how she treated loyal servants. Within a fortnight, she was alone, her followers scattered or turned against her. She left the coast and never returned."

Arthur stared at him. "You ended a dragon siege without bloodshed."

"Without *human* bloodshed. Terreth died in the confrontation. I have always regretted that." Merlin's eyes were distant. "But the villages were saved. The tribute ended. And I did not have to watch good men burn."

"Why didn't you tell me this before? When I was planning?"

Merlin met his gaze.

"Because you needed to learn that you could solve impossible problems with the tools you have. My solution required magic, time, and knowledge you don't possess. Yours required courage, preparation, and cleverness. Both worked."

"Yours was better."

"Was it?" Merlin tilted his head. "Terreth is dead. Fancadonda is alive, somewhere, nursing a grudge. Your dragon is dead. It will never burn another village. Never ally with another war band. Never teach another generation of dragons to hate humans."

Arthur thought about that.

"You're saying killing was more thorough."

"I'm saying there are no clean victories, Your Majesty. Only choices with consequences we can live with." Merlin stood. "You chose blood. I chose betrayal. History will not record which was wiser."

He walked to the door, then paused.

"Pendragon. It suits you."

"It feels like a lie."

"All names are lies, Your Majesty. We become them or we don't. The choice is yours."

He left Arthur alone with the fire and the dragon's head on the wall and the weight of a name that felt like prophecy.
