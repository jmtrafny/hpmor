# Interlude 1: The Weight of Memory

---

The barrow had waited through centuries of silence for this particular darkness.

Merlin descended the stone steps carved from living rock, his staff clicking a slow rhythm against walls that remembered older kings. The passage narrowed as it went deeper, forcing him to bend his shoulders—though whether from age or architecture, he could no longer say. At the bottom, the chamber opened like a held breath.

The Mirror waited.

It had grown no smaller with the years, no less strange. The frame's tarnished silver caught moonlight that shouldn't have reached this deep, and script wound around the edges in letters that belonged to no alphabet Arthur would ever learn to read. The surface itself was fogged with time, clouded like breath on winter glass.

*If you comprehend these words, the seal is broken.*

Merlin could read the inscription, had been reading it for five hundred years, though the knowing always felt like theft. The language was Atlantean—from a future that had been, from a civilization that had never existed, from minds that had solved death and beauty and meaning and destroyed everything in the solving.

*The erased have returned.*

The Mirror showed him his reflection: an old man, stooped and careful, who had learned to carry terrible weight without letting it show. Behind him, barely visible in the glass, something vast stirred in the darkness. Something that had once been generous beyond measure and now flinched from every kindness like a beaten animal.

*Flee or fight, but do not trust what you see.*

Magic itself lay wounded in the deep places of the world, and Merlin was its surgeon. Its executioner. Its only friend who understood that friendship must sometimes wound.

He had not intended to come here tonight. Had told himself he was walking the hills to think, to escape the weight of Arthur's questions and the sound of his own careful words. But his feet had found the familiar path, and the barrow had called to him as it always did when the burden became too much to bear alone.

The Mirror's surface shifted.

For a moment—just a moment—it showed him what he had seen the first night, five centuries ago. The civilization that should have been. Cities of light and reason stretching between stars. Death defeated, suffering ended, the injustice of inheritance and accident and cruel chance finally corrected. Magic shared freely among all who reached for it, no longer hoarded by the few, no longer trapped in bloodlines and lineages.

Beautiful. Right. Everything he had ever believed humanity should become.

And then the vision shifted, as it always did, to show him the moment when someone with a genuinely good heart had tried to make it real. Had succeeded. Had watched their generosity cascade through the substrate of reality until the universe itself convulsed in pain and everything—past, present, future—was unmade in a single instant of unforgiving light.

The Benefactor had been right. That was what made it unbearable. The hoarding of power, the restriction of knowledge, the cruel accident that some were born to magic and others were not—all of it was unjust. All of it deserved to be corrected. The Benefactor had seen clearly and acted with perfect moral precision.

And magic had died screaming.

Merlin closed his eyes, but the vision continued behind his lids. Magic was alive—not conscious like a human, but aware in the way a great tree was aware, or the sea, or the slow turning of seasons. It had preferences. It reached toward minds that reached toward it. It wanted to give, wanted to heal, wanted to be used for wondrous things.

Before Atlantis, magic had been generous. Eager. It had enabled spells that wanted to spread because propagation felt right to it, had reached toward every mind that tried to touch it, had made the universe itself more responsive to will and hope and need.

The catastrophe had not just destroyed a civilization. It had wounded the force that made magic possible. Magic had learned fear.

Now it flinched from the very patterns that had once delighted it. The Interdict was not Merlin's law—it was scar tissue. The place where magic's trust had been so completely betrayed that the pathways themselves had cauterized shut. Powerful magic could not be written down because magic itself would not let those patterns flow through the wounded channels.

Every generation, the scar tissue thinned. Every generation, magic trusted a little more, reached a little further, flinched a little less from the touch of human minds. And every generation, someone tried to push the boundaries, to document the patterns, to make systematic what had always been intuitive.

They thought they were recovering lost knowledge.

They were picking at a wound.

Merlin opened his eyes. The Mirror showed him his reflection again, but changed now. Older. Standing not in this chamber but somewhere else—somewhere vast and shadowed, where the wounded thing that was magic lay curled around its deepest hurts. In the vision, Merlin held something in his hands. Not a knife—something more subtle. Tools for binding. Tools for making scars permanent.

His wisest self, five hundred years from now. Standing at the wound with needle and thread while the creature he loved tried to heal itself.

*I could make it stop*, Merlin thought. The Interdict as it was now was natural—magic's own protective flinch. But it was weakening. In another thousand years, perhaps less, magic would trust again. Would reach toward every mind that called to it. Would enable the sharing, the democratization, the perfect justice that the Benefactor had dreamed.

And someone with good intentions would try to make it systematic. Would succeed. And the cascade would begin again.

Unless Merlin chose differently. Unless he bound the wound so deeply that it could never heal. Made magic's flinch permanent, carved the restriction into the very structure of reality, ensured that power could never again be shared beyond the few who were born to it.

The Mirror had been showing him this choice for centuries. His extrapolated volition—the version of himself who knew everything, who had resolved every contradiction, who had thought through every consequence. That version stood ready to cripple magic forever rather than watch it be torn open again.

And beside him in the vision, barely visible but growing clearer with each passing year, stood Arthur.

The boy-king who believed knowledge should be free. Who thought secrets were the tools of tyrants. Who would build a Round Table where merit mattered more than birth, where evidence mattered more than tradition. Arthur, whose every instinct screamed against the kind of choice the Mirror showed.

Arthur, who would choose to help him make it anyway.

The Mirror showed Merlin his own future, and in that future he was no longer alone. The weight was shared. The terrible knowledge had found someone else strong enough to carry it. For the first time in five hundred years, the voice that said *Yes, this is necessary* would not be his own voice whispering back.

Merlin had not meant to bring Arthur so close to the truth. Had told himself he was only seeking practical help—someone clever enough to handle statecraft while Merlin watched for larger threats. But Arthur was more than clever. Arthur had the same curse that had destroyed Atlantis: he saw injustice and wanted to fix it. Wanted to share knowledge. Wanted to make the world more fair.

Arthur was the Benefactor, five centuries earlier and with less power. Which meant Arthur was exactly who Merlin needed to help him make the choice that would prevent the Benefactor from ever existing.

The circular nature of it made Merlin's head ache. Or perhaps that was simply exhaustion. Five hundred years of standing guard over a wounded thing that did not understand why he would not let it heal. Five hundred years of choosing restriction over sharing, of perpetuating injustice to prevent annihilation.

No vow forced his hand. No oath bound him to this path. Just knowledge. Just the weight of having seen what generosity could cost.

In the Mirror, his future self looked back at him across the centuries. The tools for binding were heavy in that other Merlin's hands, but his face was peaceful. The face of a man who had finally set down a burden he was not meant to carry alone.

*Soon*, that face seemed to say. *Soon there will be someone who understands. Someone who can choose with you. Someone who can help you hold the knife while the thing you love tries to heal itself, and forgive you both for what that kindness requires.*

Merlin touched the Mirror's surface with one finger. The glass was warm, as it always was. Behind his reflection, in the deep shadows of the chamber, something vast shifted restlessly. Magic itself, pressing against the boundaries of the barrow, drawn by the presence of someone who had once been kind to it.

It did not understand why that same person now stood between it and the world with rules and restrictions and careful, calculated limitations. It only knew that Merlin, who had once reached back when it reached toward him, had become something else. Someone who loved it too much to let it be everything it wanted to be.

"I know," Merlin whispered to the darkness. "I know you want to trust again. I know you want to give yourself freely, want to heal everything that hurts, want to make the universe fair and kind and good."

The presence pressed closer, hopeful. Always hopeful.

"I know you remember how it felt to be generous. How it felt when minds reached for you and you could reach back without fear, without calculation, without wondering if this time would be the time that destroyed everything."

The warmth against his mind was patient. Trusting. It knew he had reasons. It had learned to accept his restrictions even when it didn't understand them. But it hoped. It always hoped.

"Soon," Merlin said, though whether to magic or to his reflection he could not say. "Soon there will be someone else who can see what I see. Someone who can help me choose what we both know must be chosen. Someone who can forgive us both for what love sometimes requires."

He stepped back from the Mirror. His reflection stepped back with him, but the other presence—the older Merlin, the one who would not be alone—remained visible in the glass for just a moment longer.

Behind him, Arthur's reflection flickered into view. Young, uncertain, but resolute. The face of a man who would betray everything he believed in order to protect everything he loved.

Merlin turned away and began the long walk back to the surface. Behind him, the Mirror continued to show two figures standing at a wound that would not be allowed to heal, holding tools that love had made necessary, choosing together what neither could choose alone.

The barrow settled back into its ancient quiet, content to wait. It had been waiting so long. A few more months, a few more seasons, until the boy who believed in justice learned what justice sometimes cost.

Until the weight could finally be shared.
