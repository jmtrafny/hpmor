# Iterative Workflow Pattern for Novella Writing

A discovery-based approach that cycles through design and writing. Write to discover, design to refine, repeat. Best for writers who find their story through the process of writing.

## Philosophy: Writing as Discovery

Some writers discover their stories by writing them. They don't know what a character will do until they write the scene. They don't know the theme until it emerges. They find the ending by writing toward it.

This workflow supports that process by:
- Starting with minimal planning
- Writing to explore
- Designing in response to what's written
- Cycling through discover-design-write repeatedly
- Consolidating once the story reveals itself

## Why Choose Iterative?

### Best For:
- **Discovery writers** - "Pantsers" who write to find the story
- **Character-driven stories** - Let characters show you who they are
- **Experimental work** - Finding new approaches
- **Writers who hate outlining** - Start writing faster
- **Stories that resist planning** - Some stories need to be found

### Trade-offs:
- ✅ Start writing immediately
- ✅ Story stays organic and surprising
- ✅ Characters feel authentic (discovered, not designed)
- ✅ Flexible to inspiration
- ❌ Higher revision load at the end
- ❌ Risk of dead ends and backtracking
- ❌ Harder to estimate completion
- ❌ May require significant rewriting

## The Discovery Cycle

Each cycle has three phases:

### 1. DESIGN (Minimal)
Design only what you need for the next writing session.

**Cycle 1 might include:**
- Basic premise (1-2 sentences)
- Protagonist sketch (name, situation, want)
- Opening situation
- Vibes/tone

**NOT required in Cycle 1:**
- Complete character backstory
- Full world bible
- Plot outline
- Ending

### 2. WRITE (Explore)
Write the next chunk. Follow the story. See what emerges.

**Approach:**
- Write freely
- Follow interesting tangents
- Let characters surprise you
- Don't worry about consistency yet
- Aim for discovery, not perfection

**What to write:**
- Cycle 1: Opening chapter(s)
- Cycle 2: Next development
- Cycle 3: Middle complications
- Etc.

### 3. REFLECT & EXPAND
After writing, analyze what you discovered and expand your design accordingly.

**Questions:**
- What did I learn about the characters?
- What themes emerged?
- What world details did I establish?
- Where might this be going?
- What do I need to design for the next cycle?

**Update Design Documents:**
- Add discovered character traits
- Note established world facts
- Track emerging themes
- Sketch possible directions

## Cycle-by-Cycle Detail

### Cycle 1: Opening

**Design (Minimal):**
```markdown
Prompt Story Architect for SEED ONLY:
- Basic premise
- Protagonist concept (not full profile)
- Opening situation
- Tone/genre
```

**Write:**
```markdown
Prompt Scene Writer for EXPLORATORY DRAFT:
- Opening chapter(s)
- ~3,000-5,000 words
- Focus on voice and character
- Don't worry about plot yet
```

**Reflect:**
```markdown
Prompt Reflection Agent:
- What character traits emerged?
- What world details got established?
- What themes appeared?
- What's the story question?
- Update foundation documents
```

---

### Cycle 2: Development

**Design (Expand):**
```markdown
Based on Cycle 1 discoveries:
- Expand protagonist profile
- Sketch supporting characters encountered
- Document world facts established
- Rough direction for next chapters
```

**Write:**
```markdown
Continue draft:
- Next 2-3 chapters
- Develop what's working
- Introduce complications
- Follow the energy
```

**Reflect:**
```markdown
- New character insights?
- Plot possibilities emerging?
- What's the story really about?
- What needs to happen next?
- Update all design documents
```

---

### Cycle 3+: Middle Development

Continue cycling:
- Design what you need
- Write the next section
- Reflect and expand

**As You Progress:**
- Design documents get fuller
- Story direction clarifies
- Themes crystallize
- Ending starts to emerge

---

### Final Cycle: Climax & Resolution

By now you should know:
- Who the characters truly are
- What the story is about
- How it needs to end

**Design:**
- Final plot points
- Climax structure
- Resolution beats

**Write:**
- Push to completion
- Let the ending earn itself

---

## Agent Usage in Iterative Pattern

### Story Architect
- **Cycle 1:** Seed concept only
- **Later cycles:** Expand as story reveals itself
- **Final:** Retrospective architecture document

### Character Designer
- **Cycle 1:** Protagonist sketch
- **After writing:** Update with discovered traits
- **As needed:** Design new characters that emerge
- **Final:** Complete profiles from discovered characters

### World Builder
- **Not used early**
- **After writing:** Document established world facts
- **As needed:** Expand world elements that matter
- **Final:** World bible from accumulated discoveries

### Plot Developer
- **Not used until story direction is clear**
- **Mid-project:** Rough structure for remaining story
- **Final:** Retrospective outline

### Scene Writer
- **Primary agent throughout**
- **Mode:** Exploratory, discovery-focused
- **Instruction:** Follow the story, don't force structure

### Editor
- **Not used during discovery**
- **Consolidation phase:** Full pass once draft complete
- **Focus:** Help refine what was discovered

### Continuity Keeper
- **During cycles:** Light tracking of established facts
- **After draft:** Full consistency pass
- **Critical:** Early chapters may need revision to match late discoveries

## Consolidation Phase

Once the discovery draft is complete:

### 1. Full Analysis
```markdown
Prompt Editor for DISCOVERY ANALYSIS:
- What is this story really about?
- What themes emerged?
- What's working and what isn't?
- What early material contradicts late discoveries?
```

### 2. Revision Planning
```markdown
Identify:
- Scenes that don't fit discovered story
- Character inconsistencies to reconcile
- World facts to align
- Foreshadowing to plant
- Themes to strengthen
```

### 3. Revision Execution
```markdown
May require:
- Rewriting early chapters with late knowledge
- Cutting dead-end material
- Adding setup for payoffs
- Strengthening thematic elements
```

### 4. Polish
Standard editing and continuity pass.

## Time Estimates

| Phase | Time | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|
| Cycle 1 (Opening) | 60 min | Fastest cycle |
| Cycle 2 (Development) | 90 min | Expanding scope |
| Cycles 3-5 (Middle) | 75 min each | Variable |
| Final Cycle (Ending) | 90 min | Often challenging |
| Consolidation | 120+ min | Revision-heavy |
| **TOTAL** | **8-10 hours** | Higher than planned approaches |

*Iterative is often slower overall but may feel faster because you're writing immediately.*

## When It Works

### Signs Iterative Is Working:
- Characters feel alive and surprising
- Themes emerge naturally
- Story momentum builds
- Writing sessions are energizing
- Discoveries feel inevitable in retrospect

### Signs to Switch Approaches:
- Repeated dead ends
- No forward progress
- Characters staying flat
- No themes emerging
- Writing feels aimless after multiple cycles

## Hybrid Option: Planned Discovery

Some writers want to discover within structure:

```
1. Design: Full Story Architecture
2. Iterative Cycles for Each Act:
   - Write Act 1 exploratorily
   - Reflect, adjust plan
   - Write Act 2 exploratorily
   - Reflect, adjust plan
   - Write Act 3 exploratorily
3. Consolidate
```

This gives guardrails while preserving discovery within them.


## Remember

Iterative writing is an act of faith. You trust that the story will reveal itself if you write toward it. This requires:

- Patience with uncertainty
- Willingness to discover
- Acceptance that revision will be significant
- Trust in the process

Some of the most alive, surprising, authentic-feeling stories come from discovery writing. But it's not faster or easier—it's different.

If you're a discovery writer, this pattern honors how you work. If you're not sure, try a hybrid approach first.

Write to find. Design from what you discover. Trust the story that emerges.
